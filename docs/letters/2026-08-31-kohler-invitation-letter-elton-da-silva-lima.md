**Subject: Invitation to host your ETH Fellows postdoctoral project at Gramazio Kohler Research**

Dear Elton,

Prof. Hölscher recommended your work to me, and since then Dr. Xydis has had the chance to go through your proposal with you directly. Having now read it closely myself, I wanted to write to you in my own words about why I would be glad to host you at Gramazio Kohler Research for your ETH Fellows application.

Your proposal takes on a question that, as far as I know, no one has yet answered with real rigor: does replacing the scalar scattering coefficient with a full directional, bidirectional representation change which architectural alternatives we would actually choose — and if so, how much angular and spectral detail do we need before we can trust that decision? This is only answerable now, thanks to very recent developments: Heimes and Vorländer's method for measuring and representing bidirectional scattering coefficients, the interlaboratory dataset that followed it, and the new Sparrowpy tool, which for the first time allows both representations to be run and compared within the same simulation environment. You are proposing to ask this question at precisely the moment it has become possible to answer it, with a design careful enough to produce a real result: paired simulations that isolate the representation as the only variable, a robustness check against the uncertainty in the bidirectional data, and a clear path from that comparison to an interpretable, testable decision criterion. This is exactly the kind of applied, decision-relevant acoustics research I want to see more of in our field.

What matters just as much to me is how directly this project builds on what our group has already invested in. We built the acquisition setup and released the GIR dataset of scaled-model impulse responses some years ago, and we have since explored what that kind of data can reveal about the relationship between surface geometry and acoustic performance. Your project puts that same lineage of work to a genuinely new purpose: testing whether the extra directional detail we can now measure actually changes what an architect would build. You would also be working with Sparrowpy and the parametric Rhino and Grasshopper workflows our group already knows well, so you would not be starting from zero — this is not a proposal I would host out of general goodwill alone, but one that extends work we care about, using infrastructure we can genuinely offer.

For these reasons, I would be genuinely glad to host you as your ETH Fellow host professor. Questions like yours are exactly why I look forward to welcoming you to Gramazio Kohler Research, and I hope to have the chance to work with you on it.

With best regards,
